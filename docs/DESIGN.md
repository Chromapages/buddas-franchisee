```text

        ↓

        ↓
04  DESIGN SYSTEM / design.md
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
07  PAGE / PACKAGE / LOCATION ART DIRECTION
        ↓
08  CAMPAIGN / EXPERIMENT
```

## Hard Rule

A lower layer may interpret an upstream rule.

It may not silently rewrite it.

Examples:

- a campaign may not change the Classic Budda Roll into a glazed pastry
- a responsive layout may crop Arc Form 02 but may not redraw it
- a franchise page may become more evidentiary but may not invent a new corporate color system
- a packaging vendor may adapt a dieline but may not recreate the wordmark from a screenshot
- a restaurant architect may adapt the kit of parts to a real building but may not invent a new “Hawaiian theme” for the location

# 1. The Budda’s Design Thesis

Budda’s is not designed as:

- a generic Hawaiian restaurant
- a plate-lunch clone
- a tropical theme restaurant
- a luxury hospitality brand
- a trendy bakery café
- a playful SaaS product
- a lifestyle brand that happens to sell food

Budda’s lives in productive tensions:

```text
LOCAL SOUL              × SCALABLE DISCIPLINE
BAKERY WARMTH            × MODERN QSR CLARITY
HAWAI‘I ROOTS            × NO CULTURAL COSTUMING
PREMIUM-CASUAL           × GENEROUS
SPECIAL ENOUGH TO SEEK   × EASY ENOUGH TO REPEAT
HUMAN                    × SYSTEMATIC
MOVEMENT                 × GROUNDING
ROLL                     × TABLE
```

The desired result is:

**SOUL × SYSTEM**

The system exists so growth creates **more Budda’s**, not more variation.

---

## 1.1 The Design Constitution

These are the highest-level rules. They should survive future redesigns.

## 01 — FOOD FIRST.

If design fights the food, design loses.

## 02 — THE ROLL IS THE ICON.

The Budda Roll receives disproportionate protection and visual investment.

## 03 — PRODUCT TRUTH OUTRANKS ART DIRECTION.

A beautiful false product is a failed asset.

## 04 — HAWAI‘I IS ROOT, NOT DECORATION.

Specificity, people, food, place, memory, and relationships before visual shorthand.

## 05 — PREMIUM IS CONTROL.

More premium means better hierarchy, better photography, better materials, better usability, and fewer competing elements.

It does not mean luxury theater.

## 06 — STRUCTURE THE INFORMATION. LET THE FOOD ESCAPE.

Information obeys the grid.

Food may break it.

## 07 — THE ARC MOVES. THE TABLE GROUNDS.

Arc Form 02 creates invitation and movement.

The Table Line creates stability and continuity.

## 08 — PATTERN IS ATMOSPHERE.

Island Botanical supports the system.

It does not carry the system.

## 09 — FUNCTION FIRST. CHARACTER SECOND.

Especially for icons, wayfinding, ordering, safety information, and operational packaging.

## 10 — WARM MOTION. CONTROLLED LANDING.

Movement should reveal, settle, and get out of the way.

## 11 — CONTROL THE OUTSIDE. REWARD THE OPEN.

Packaging should be clear and confident closed; warmer and richer when opened.

## 12 — THE BAKERY OPENS INTO THE TABLE.

The physical experience should move from craving to ordering to comfort to belonging.

## 13 — ACCESSIBILITY IS HOSPITALITY.

No guest should have to fight the design.

## 14 — PERFORMANCE IS PERCEIVED QUALITY.

A premium-looking slow experience is not premium.

## 15 — EVIDENCE BEFORE ADJECTIVES.

Show what is true.

## 16 — ONE IDEA PER FRAME.

Do not make every component explain the whole brand.

## 17 — SCALE THE SYSTEM. DON’T DILUTE THE SIGNAL.

Consistency should increase with growth.

Good:

```text

+ Cream
+ Arc Form 02
+ wordmark
```

```text

+ descriptor
+ Arc

+ product marker
+ Gold

+ badge
+ CTA
+ second headline
```

Budda’s has one identity with controlled expressions.

Table = comfort / hospitality / sharing.

### Use

- cross-channel identity

### Visual formula

```text

+ Dark Teal structure
+ real food
+ Script wordmark
+ Arc Form 02
+ Table Line
+ restrained pattern
+ generous negative space
```

---

## 3.2 The Roll Signature — Product / Advertising Mode

## Purpose

Craving, product ownership, product launches, hero moments, billboards, social hooks.

## Attitude

**LOUD FOOD. QUIET DESIGN.**

## Typical assets

- one major Product Truth image
- Cream or Dark Teal
- one short Poppins message
- Arc Form 02
- small wordmark
- minimal support

## Avoid

- dense copy
- many cards
- full-strength pattern
- excessive illustration
- operational information

---

## 3.3 The Island Ledger — Story / Editorial / Evidence Mode

## Purpose

Origin, La‘ie, family story, community, culture, franchise proof, history, editorial storytelling.

## Typical assets

- Cream + Dark Teal
- Poppins headline
- DM Sans body
- date / place metadata
- Gold micro-detail
- Table Line
- documentary photography or Island Ledger Illustration
- restrained Arc
- Ghost Island Botanical where useful

## Tone

Specific.

Grounded.

Warm.

Documentary.

Never faux-vintage.

---

## 3.4 The Bakery Counter — Functional / Operational Mode

## Purpose

Ordering, menus, catering configuration, forms, franchise disclosures, wayfinding, pickup, utility.

## Attitude

**CLARITY IS THE BRAND.**

## Typical assets

- Cream / White
- Dark Teal hierarchy
- Base Teal recognition
- Poppins + DM Sans
- Budda’s Utility Line
- Table Line
- minimal Arc
- minimal pattern

## Rule

Functional design is not a weaker expression of Budda’s.

It proves that the brand is capable.

---

## 3.5 Mode Mixing

Good:

```text
Roll Signature hero
→ Bakery Counter ordering
→ Island Ledger story
→ Roll & Table conversion close
```

Good:

```text
Generous Table catering hero
→ Bakery Counter packages
→ Island Ledger credibility
```

Avoid combining all modes within one small component.

# 4. Design Token Architecture

Budda’s tokens should separate:

```text
PRIMITIVES
what exists
    ↓
SEMANTICS
what it means
    ↓
COMPONENT TOKENS
how it is consumed
```

Do not scatter raw color, spacing, radius, motion, and typography values through component code.

The token architecture should remain compatible with the stable DTCG 2025.10 model for interoperable design tokens. As of August 2026, DTCG 2025.10 remains the stable published version; later drafts may exist, but this file targets the stable report until a new stable release is adopted.

## 5.2 Color Roles

## Base Teal

Use for:

- primary brand recognition
- official wordmark on light surfaces
- Arc Form 02
- selected icons
- active states where contrast works
- packaging recognition
- environmental moments

Do not use it as the default surface for long light-colored body copy.

## Dark Teal

Use for:

- structural fields
- primary digital actions
- headings
- footer / header
- editorial fields
- menu hierarchy
- environmental anchors
- wayfinding

Dark Teal communicates **capability**, not heaviness.

## Cream

The emotional neutral.

Use for:

- websites
- menus
- packaging
- story
- restaurant walls
- brand surfaces
- collateral

## Cocoa

Warm ink.

Use for:

- body copy
- packaging text
- bakery storytelling
- warm neutrals

## Gold

**DETAIL, NOT FIELD.**

Use as jewelry:

- small rule
- metadata
- premium micro-detail
- select hardware / editorial annotation

## Orange

**PAY ATTENTION NOW.**

Use only for:

- NEW
- LIMITED
- opening
- time-sensitive promotion
- temporary signal

Orange is not a generic fun color.

---

## 5.3 Brand Color Ratio

Recommended overall visual direction:

```text
55–65%  Cream + White
20–30%  Base Teal + Dark Teal
5–10%   Gold + Orange
```

This is a discipline, not a mathematical quota.

Food should often be the most colorful thing in the frame.

## 7.7 Mobile Principle

**LESS GEOMETRY. MORE IMPACT.**

On mobile:

- food stays large
- typography stays readable
- Arc Form 02 crops harder
- Island Botanical reduces
- secondary decoration disappears early
- actions become simpler
- content stacks deliberately
- horizontal concepts become horizontal only when the job benefits from it

Do not simply scale desktop.

---

## 7.8 Responsive Degradation Order

When space becomes constrained:

1. remove nonessential decoration
2. reduce pattern
3. crop Arc Form 02 more aggressively
4. art-direct the image differently
5. simplify supporting copy
6. stack layout
7. convert genuinely secondary disclosure into an accessible accordion or progressive disclosure

Never:

- distort the wordmark
- distort food
- thin Arc Form 02
- hide the primary action
- make body text tiny
- make focus invisible
- let sticky UI completely hide focused controls
- sacrifice product truth to preserve a composition

# 9. Radius, Borders, Lines + Elevation

## 9.1 Radius Philosophy

Budda’s is soft without becoming bubbly.

The Roll and Script already provide significant softness.

UI should use moderate radii.

```text
radius.xs    6px
radius.sm    8px
radius.md   12px
radius.lg   16px
radius.xl   24px
radius.2xl  32px
radius.pill 999px
```

Pill is reserved for:

- tags
- chips
- segmented control items
- status pills

Not every button or card.

---

## 9.2 Borders

Use quiet structural borders.

Typical:

- 1px default
- 2px selected / focus-related visual state when required
- Dark Teal-derived low-opacity neutral

Do not box every section.

## 9.3 Section Divider

Use the `section-divider` primitive for a quiet structural break within a public
section:

```text
border    1px Dark Teal at 10% opacity
padding   24px between the rule and the following content
spacing   parent layout owns the preceding gap; use space.7 (32px) compact or space.8 (40px) desktop
```

Do not add a local border color or padding when this primitive is used. Use a
stronger divider only when the component documents a semantic reason.

---

## 9.4 Shadows

Acceptable:

- natural food contact shadow
- subtle card elevation
- modal / drawer elevation

Avoid:

- floating-everything UI
- text shadows
- glowing buttons
- logo glow
- fake product shadow detached from the food

# 10. Master Composition System

## 10.1 Visual Hierarchy — Locked

```text
01  FOOD
02  MESSAGE
03  BRAND
04  ACTION
05  DECORATION
```

If Island Botanical is noticed before the Budda Roll:

the composition failed.

---

## 10.2 Master Composition Principle

**STRUCTURED ENOUGH TO TRUST. LOOSE ENOUGH TO CRAVE.**

Information follows the grid.

Food may:

- bleed
- overlap
- cross columns
- leave frame
- cover part of the Arc

The tension is intentional.

---

## 10.3 Food-to-Graphic Ratio

Consumer-facing starting point:

```text
60–70%  food / product impact
20–30%  negative space / brand field
10–15%  type / branding
```

Not a literal measurement.

A reminder that graphics frame appetite.

---

## 10.4 The 70/30 Attention Rule

Approximately 70% of visual attention should often belong to one dominant thing.

Usually:

**THE FOOD.**

Do not create six elements at equal weight.

---

## 10.5 Negative Space

Negative space is a brand asset.

Cream can remain empty.

Dark Teal can remain quiet.

An unused area is not unfinished when it strengthens hierarchy.

---

## 10.6 Alignment

Budda’s favors:

**ASYMMETRY WITH CONTROL.**

Common pattern:

- left-aligned type
- right-weighted food
- controlled crop
- balanced whitespace

Centering is useful for:

- logo pages
- Roll Box lids
- select formal story moments
- app / micro marks
- one-line product claims

Do not center every composition.

---

## 10.7 Food Crop Levels

## Level 01 — Complete

Ordering / utility.

## Level 02 — Hero

Approximately 10–20% crop.

## Level 03 — Macro

Approximately 30–60% crop.

## Level 04 — Abstract Food

Extreme texture.

Use sparingly.

### Rule

Never crop away the product characteristic the message depends on.

---

## 10.8 Composition Recipes — Locked

## CRAVE

Food + Arc + short headline + wordmark.

## SHARE

One hero + supporting foods + hands / table + Table Line.

## STORY

Image / illustration + editorial type + specific metadata + Gold detail.

## ORDER

Structured type + Utility Line + Table Line + minimal imagery.

## PROMOTE

Food + short Orange signal + Dark Teal + action.

These recipes apply across digital, physical, packaging, and campaign systems.

# 12. Roll Arc Form 02

## 12.1 Status

**ARC FORM 02 ONLY — LOCKED**

Forms 01 and 03 are not active core geometry.

---

## 12.2 Definition

A bold, soft, cropped arch derived from Budda’s product language and used for:

- movement
- framing
- recognition
- reveal
- edge behavior

It is not a secondary logo.

It is not:

- rainbow
- wave
- sunrise
- bridge
- wellness icon
- surf graphic

---

## 12.3 Master Geometry

Normalized theoretical master:

```text
Width              1000 units
Rise               ~420–460 units
Width : rise       ~2.25 : 1
Band thickness     ~105–125 units
```

The curve is wide, shallow, slightly asymmetrical, and softly resolved.

It should feel baked rather than CAD-perfect.

---

## 12.4 Default Crop

**LOWER-RIGHT**

Approved:

- upper-right
- lower-left
- full-bleed side sweep

Typical crop:

25–45% of theoretical form may remain off-canvas.

---

## 12.5 Responsive Visibility

```text
Desktop  55–80%
Tablet   45–70%
Mobile   25–55%
```

On smaller screens:

show less Arc.

Do not create a thinner Arc.

---

## 12.6 Food Relationship

Preferred:

**FOOD OVER ARC.**

Approximately 15–45% of the visible Arc may be obscured by food when compositionally useful.

The Arc may echo the Roll.

It may not trace the Roll.

---

## 12.7 Safe Zone

Let `R` = Arc band thickness.

```text
Text/logo minimum      1R
Preferred              1.5R
Wordmark preferred     ≥1.5R
```

Food may intentionally break this zone.

Information generally should not.

---

## 12.8 Color

Priority:

1. Base Teal
2. Dark Teal
3. Cream reverse
4. Cocoa rare
5. Orange only for genuine promotional use
6. Gold extremely rare

---

## 12.9 Layered Arc

Optional special treatment:

- maximum two brand bands
- one negative-space gap

Never build rainbow stacks.

---

## 12.10 Implementation

Use one official vector master.

Decorative web Arc:

```html
<div class="bds-arc" aria-hidden="true">
  <svg><!-- approved master path --></svg>
</div>
```

Use layout, mask, clipping, or transforms to crop.

Do not recreate with `border-radius`.

# 13. Table Line

## 13.1 Definition

A simple, substantial horizontal line that grounds composition and represents:

- table
- counter
- service
- sharing
- continuity

Arc says:

**COME IN.**

Table Line says:

**STAY HERE.**

---

## 13.2 Geometry

Let `R` = Arc band thickness.

```text
Table thickness T ≈ 0.16R–0.24R
```

Default line length:

```text
40–75% of composition width
```

Use soft rounded caps.

---

## 13.3 Use

Good:

- category grounding
- hero base
- menu anchor
- story divider
- evidence section
- packaging edge
- counter fascia

Avoid:

- underline under every headline
- dashed line
- decorative endpoints
- wave forms
- arbitrary vertical use

A vertical divider is a divider.

It is not the Table Line.

# 15. Iconography — Budda’s Utility Line

## 15.1 Status

**LOCKED — IDENTITY 07**

The previous provisional icon rules are retired.

The functional system is:

**BUDDA’S UTILITY LINE**

---

## 15.2 Master Principle

**FUNCTION FIRST. CHARACTER SECOND.**

An icon must first be understood.

Brand character comes through:

- stroke
- softness
- color
- geometry
- spacing
- motion

Not semantic reinvention.

---

## 15.3 Character

Icons should feel:

**SOFT + STURDY + CLEAR**

Avoid:

- hairlines
- luxury-minimal thin strokes
- cartoonish icons
- hyper-geometric tech symbols
- emoji character
- decorative tropical symbols

---

## 15.4 Master Grid

```text
Artboard             24 × 24px
Active drawing area  20 × 20px
Optical breathing    ~2px each side
```

---

## 15.5 Size Family

| Application | Icon Size |
|---|---:|
| Micro UI | 16px |
| Standard UI | 24px |
| Large UI / Menu | 32px |
| Signage | 48px |
| Environmental | 64px+ |

Do not simply scale the 24px master indefinitely.

Use optical corrections.

---

## 15.6 Stroke

```text
16px  ~1.5px
24px   2px MASTER
32px  ~2.5px
48px  ~3–3.5px
```

No hairlines.

---

## 15.7 Terminals + Joins

Use:

**ROUND CAPS**

and:

**ROUND / SOFTENED JOINS**

where geometry permits.

Do not make everything a blob.

Clarity remains primary.

---

## 15.8 Geometry

Construct primarily from:

- circles
- soft rectangles
- straight horizontal / vertical lines
- broad arcs
- simple diagonals

The Arc influences family resemblance.

Do not paste Arc Form 02 into every icon.

---

## 15.9 Detail Budget

At 24px:

- one primary idea
- maximum two secondary details

If more detail is needed:

it may be an illustration rather than an icon.

**Cream**

secondary editorial / bakery use.

**OUTLINE**

## 18.3 Camera Language

Preferred food range:

```text
70–110mm full-frame equivalent
```

Classic Budda Roll sweet spot:

**~85mm**

Table / broader environment:

```text
45–65mm where needed
```

Macro:

```text
90–120mm macro character
```

Avoid close ultra-wide food photography that changes product proportions.

---

## 18.4 Aperture

Preferred hero range:

**f/4–f/8**

depending on sensor, distance, lens, and product depth.

Product comprehension before cinematic blur.

---

## 18.5 Lighting

**LARGE + SOFT + DIRECTIONAL**

Starting logic:

- large key
- restrained fill
- optional negative fill
- physically logical contact shadow
- natural material highlights

Do not apply one generic “food shine.”

Bread, glaze, gravy, egg, fried crust, and metal all behave differently.

## 18.6 Color Grade

**WARM + NATURAL + RICH + CONTROLLED**

No aggressive teal/orange cinematic grade.

No orange bread.

No cyan shadow wash.

No crushed black texture.

No artificial HDR.

**BRAND THE WORLD. DO NOT RECOLOR THE FOOD.**

## 18.7 Surface System

## 18.10 Human Photography

# 20. Motion + Interaction System

## 20.1 Status

**LOCKED — IDENTITY 08**

The provisional motion system in v1.0 is retired.

---

## 20.2 Motion North Star

**WARM MOTION. CONTROLLED LANDING.**

Movement begins with energy.

Then settles.

It should feel like:

- a box arriving
- a tray landing
- a Roll opening
- food being passed
- a guest moving through the experience

---

## 20.3 Master Grammar

**REVEAL → LAND → SHARE**

## Reveal

Something becomes available.

## Land

The composition becomes stable.

## Share

The user can continue, act, pass, open, order, or move forward.

---

## 20.4 Product-Derived Motion Verbs

Approved source behaviors:

- open
- pull
- tear
- lift
- place
- pass
- serve
- share

Budda’s motion comes from food and hospitality.

Not from tropical motion clichés.

---

## 20.5 Motion Hierarchy

```text
01  FOOD
02  MESSAGE
03  BRAND STRUCTURE
04  ACTION
05  DECORATION
```

Motion obeys visual hierarchy.

If decoration competes with menu comprehension:

stop the decoration.

---

## 20.6 Motion Intensities

## Level 01 — Utility

Fast.

Quiet.

Functional.

Use for:

- buttons
- menus
- accordions
- forms
- ordering
- cart

## Level 02 — Brand

Noticeable but restrained.

Use for:

- page entry
- hero reveal
- image transition
- Arc
- Table Line
- Island Ledger

## Level 03 — Campaign

Most expressive.

Use for:

- Budda Roll advertising
- brand film
- social video
- product launch
- digital signage

Checkout does not move like an ad.

---

## 20.7 Locked Duration Tokens

```text
motion-instant      100ms
motion-quick        160ms
motion-standard     240ms
motion-emphasis     360ms
motion-section      560ms
motion-brand        900ms
motion-cinematic    1200–1400ms max
```

**USE THE SHORTEST DURATION THAT COMMUNICATES THE MOTION CLEARLY.**

---

## 20.9 Locked Easing

```css
:root {
  --bds-ease-out: cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1);
  --bds-ease-standard: cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1);
  --bds-ease-inout: cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1);
}
```

Use:

- ease-out for arrival
- ease-in-out for state change

Do not use:

- elastic
- rubber
- bounce
- overshoot

---

## 20.10 Motion Distance

```text
Micro interaction  2–4px
Component          4–8px
Section            16–32px max
Brand graphic      may travel farther if entering off-canvas
```

Do not slide every headline 80px upward.

---

## 20.11 Scale

Typical:

```text
0.98 → 1
```

or:

```text
1 → 0.985
```

for press.

Avoid:

- 0.8 → 1
- 1 → 1.1
- pulsing
- inflating food

---

## 20.12 Arc Reveal

Primary brand motion.

Behavior:

- begins 20–45% outside frame
- enters smoothly
- stops decisively
- remains cropped
- keeps full thickness
- never bounces
- never completes into a perfect rainbow

Duration:

**500–700ms**

---

## 20.13 Arc Sweep

Use for:

- image transition
- campaign transition
- selected section transition

Sequence:

```text

↓
MASKS / COVERS
↓
NEW CONTENT REVEALS
↓
ARC CONTINUES
↓
ARC STOPS PARTLY OFF-CANVAS
```

One gesture.

One job.

---

## 20.14 Table Line Land

The line extends horizontally.

Usually:

left → right.

Duration:

**350–500ms**

Then content settles above it.

---

## 20.15 The Pull Motion

Sequence:

```text
CONTACT
↓
TENSION
↓
EARLY SEPARATION
↓
CRUMB REVEAL
↓
FINAL BREAK
↓
SETTLE
```

Food physics remain real.

---

## 20.16 Box Open

Sequence:

```text
BOX ARRIVES
↓
LID OPENS
↓
ROLLS REVEAL
↓
HOLD
```

No digital explosion.

No confetti.

The reveal is the reward.

---

## 20.17 The Handoff

A product passes between people.

Use for:

- pickup
- catering
- hospitality
- community
- advertising

The handoff embodies sharing without requiring a slogan.

---

## 20.18 Wordmark Motion

Preferred:

**MASK REVEAL**

Duration:

```text
700–950ms
```

Use only in high-value brand moments.

Do not run every page load.

No spinning.

No bouncing letters.

No morphing.

---

## 20.19 Island Botanical Motion

Default:

**STATIC**

If editorial motion is justified:

- very slow 4–8px drift
- restrained layer reveal

No looping leaf dance.

---

## 20.20 Island Ledger Motion

Use:

- mask reveal
- layer fade
- 8–16px translation
- metadata entry
- short line draw

Typical:

```text
450–700ms
stagger 40–70ms
max ~3–4 layers
```

Editorial dignity over spectacle.

---

## 20.21 Bakery Counter Motion

Fast and nearly invisible.

Typical:

```text
100–280ms
```

The user should experience speed because the system is well designed.

---

## 20.22 Scroll Reveal

Do not animate every paragraph.

Default:

- major sections only
- 8–24px travel
- 450–600ms
- once per meaningful entry

The website is not an animation reel.

---

## 20.23 Parallax

Rare.

Maximum approximate travel:

```text
16–24px over a large scroll distance
```

Never use heavy parallax on:

- food
- ordering
- body text
- forms

---

## 20.24 Hero Motion Sequence

Core content should be usable immediately.

Recommended pattern:

```text
0ms      core content available
80ms     food begins subtle reveal
160ms    headline settles
260ms    CTA appears
320ms    Arc enters
650ms    Table Line lands
```

Do not hide the interface behind animation.

---

## 20.25 No Mandatory Splash Screen

Do not manufacture waiting to show branding.

A loader is allowed only when the interface genuinely needs to wait.

**HOSPITALITY WITHOUT FRICTION.**

---

## 20.26 Reduced Motion — Required

When `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:

- remove Arc sweeps
- remove parallax
- remove large translation
- remove decorative auto-motion
- replace motion with direct state change or small opacity transition
- use static posters for optional video

Recommended necessary transition:

```text
80–160ms
```

No information or state may depend on motion alone.

---

## 20.27 Motion Technology Hierarchy

## CSS

Hover, press, basic transitions.

## Web Animations / Motion Library

Component choreography / complex state.

## SVG

Arc, Table Line, icon stroke, wordmark mask.

## Lottie / Rive

Only for genuinely reusable complex brand sequences.

## Video

Real food, real people, real process.

The more physical the subject:

the more video should carry it.

# 21. Interaction System

## 21.1 Interaction Principle

A Budda’s interaction should answer:

**WHAT HAPPENED?**

then:

**WHAT CAN I DO NEXT?**

Motion may clarify.

It may not be the only answer.

---

## 21.2 Required Component States

Every interactive component must define:

- default
- hover where applicable
- pressed
- focus-visible
- selected
- disabled
- loading where applicable
- error where applicable
- success where applicable

No component ships with only a default state.

---

## 21.3 Buttons

## Hover

140–180ms.

Optional:

```text
translateY(-1px)
```

## Press

80–120ms.

Optional:

```text
translateY(1px)
```

or:

```text
scale(.985)
```

No bounce.

---

## 21.4 Primary CTA

Default:

- Dark Teal
- Cream text
- Poppins SemiBold
- height 48–56px
- horizontal padding 20–28px
- radius 12–16px

A directional icon may move 2–4px.

The CTA should feel:

**READY.**

Not desperate for attention.

---

## 21.5 Secondary CTA

- transparent / Cream field
- Dark Teal text
- Dark Teal border
- same height as paired primary

---

## 21.6 Promotional CTA

Orange is allowed only when temporary urgency is real.

Promotion is not the default brand interaction.

---

## 21.7 Navigation Motion

Desktop:

```text
160–200ms
```

Dropdown:

```text
180–240ms
```

Mobile drawer:

```text
260–320ms
```

Do not make the logo morph on routine scroll.

---

## 21.8 Mobile Menu

Links may stagger:

```text
20–35ms max per row
```

Do not create a slow cascading menu.

Navigation is utility.

---

## 21.9 Accordion

```text
220–280ms
```

Use:

- real button
- `aria-expanded`
- clear open state
- accessible content order

Do not hide essential conversion information merely for visual cleanliness.

---

## 21.10 Product Cards

Hover may use:

- 0–2px lift
- restrained elevation
- image scale max ~1.015
- crop shift / alternate product image where useful

Do not spin food.

Do not generate fake steam on hover.

Do not jump the card dramatically.

---

## 21.11 Cart

Add confirmation should be clear through:

- button state
- cart count
- short toast
- optional icon change

Approximate feedback:

```text
180–260ms
```

No confetti.

---

## 21.12 Success States

Confident + complete.

Use:

- checkmark
- short fill transition
- clear text
- next useful action

---

## 21.13 Error States

Direct and respectful.

Use:

- clear message
- persistent visual state
- field association
- color + icon / text

Optional one-time 2–4px correction motion.

No repeated shaking.

---

## 21.14 Loading

## Micro

Spinner / short progress.

## Content

Skeleton preserving final geometry.

## Brand

Only for real extended wait.

If a branded loader is justified:

use Arc Reveal or Script B simply.

---

## 21.15 Touch

Press feedback should begin within approximately 100ms.

Do not rely on hover.

Touch should feel connected to the finger.

# 22. Packaging + Physical Brand System

## 22.1 Physical North Star — **LOCKED**

**GENEROUSLY REVEALED.**

Packaging is not a surface on which the identity is printed.

It is a customer experience with two stages:

## CLOSED

Controlled.

Clear.

Confident.

Recognizable.

## OPEN

Warmer.

More expressive.

More story.

More food.

More reward.

The master principle is:

**CONTROLLED OUTSIDE. EXPRESSIVE INSIDE.**

---

## 22.2 Packaging Has Five Jobs

Every package must:

### 01 — PROTECT

Preserve food quality.

### 02 — ORGANIZE

Make carrying and unpacking intuitive.

### 03 — IDENTIFY

Clearly communicate Budda’s.

### 04 — INFORM

Carry useful and required information.

### 05 — DELIGHT

Create one restrained brand moment.

A package that delights but does not protect the food has failed.

---

## 22.3 Packaging Hierarchy — **LOCKED**

### Tier 01 — Icon

**BUDDA ROLL BOX**

Highest brand and experience investment.

### Tier 02 — Signature

Meals.

Bentos.

Bakery.

Catering.

### Tier 03 — Utility

Sauce.

Cutlery.

Napkin.

Cup.

Receipt.

Operational labels.

### Tier 04 — Future Extension

Retail.

Frozen.

Shipping.

Wholesale.

Only after the business is proven.

---

## 22.4 Physical Architecture

Design in this order:

```text
PRODUCT
   ↓
PACKAGE FORM
   ↓
COLOR
   ↓
WORDMARK / PRODUCT NAME
   ↓
ARC + TABLE LINE
   ↓
STORY / PATTERN / UTILITY
```

**FORM BEFORE GRAPHICS.**

A distinctive package that crushes food, traps condensation, stacks badly, or slows the line is not distinctive brand design.

It is an operational defect.

---

## 22.5 The Budda Roll Box

## Strategic Role

The Roll Box is the icon package.

The long-term goal is recognition before the wordmark is read.

Recognition should come through a combination of:

- elongated shallow form
- Cream + Teal
- official script
- Arc Form 02
- visible quantity logic
- rows of real Rolls
- interior reveal

---

### 22.5.1 Current Equity

The existing elongated shallow rectangular box and strong aqua/teal behavior are equity to evolve, not automatically discard.

Future production may become more controlled without pretending the current physical history never existed.

---

### 22.5.2 Recommended Future Master

**CREAM-DOMINANT EXTERIOR**

with:

- Base Teal recognition
- Dark Teal structure
- official wordmark
- large product / quantity information
- one Arc Form 02 gesture
- restrained Table Line

Cream is the environmental and physical hospitality canvas.

Base Teal remains visible enough to preserve recognition.

---

### 22.5.3 Quantity Architecture — **LOCKED**

```text
SINGLE = TREAT
6      = SHARE
12     = BRING
```

Quantity is not merely a pricing variable.

It changes the job the product performs.

This architecture should be reflected consistently in:

- packaging
- digital ordering
- catering
- merchandising
- photography
- campaigns

---

### 22.5.4 Lid Hierarchy

Recommended:

1. Budda’s wordmark
2. BUDDA ROLLS / product name
3. quantity
4. one Arc Form 02 gesture
5. minimum utility

Do not put the entire brand platform on the lid.

The product reveal is the reward.

---

### 22.5.5 Side Panels

Use sides for utility so the lid can remain iconic.

Potential content:

- Hawaiian Bakery & Grill
- quantity
- flavor markers
- order / web path
- one QR code
- storage / reheat information where operationally correct
- required regulatory content
- brief origin cue

---

### 22.5.6 Interior Lid

This is a high-value Island Ledger surface.

Potential hierarchy:

```text
LA‘IE, HAWAI‘I

THE ROLL THAT
STARTED EVERYTHING.

short story

Island Botanical
Gold micro-detail
Table Line
```

Story discipline:

- approximately 25–70 words
- specific rather than sentimental
- origin before generic “aloha” language
- no content that obscures the first view of the food

---

### 22.5.7 Pattern

Island Botanical is one of the strongest inside-the-box uses.

Use:

- oversized
- cropped
- tonal
- approximately Feature intensity where appropriate

Do not turn the inside lid into tiny gift-wrap wallpaper.

---

### 22.5.8 Flavor Identification

Use one master box with controlled flavor identification.

Order of preference:

1. clear Poppins flavor text
2. small approved color cue if operationally useful
3. Product Marker only when it improves sorting / comprehension

Do not build four visually unrelated package families for four flavors.

---

### 22.5.9 Mixed Boxes

Use one simple operational solution:

- exterior checkbox / mark
- small seal
- side label
- print-on-demand label

Do not turn the lid into a flavor matrix unless real customer confusion proves the need.

---

### 22.5.10 QR Code

A QR code is utility.

Rules:

- one code per package unless a legally necessary exception exists
- high contrast
- correct quiet zone
- tested at final printed size
- never integrated into the official wordmark
- never used as decorative texture

---

## 22.6 Takeout Bag

The bag is a moving sign.

It should work from across a parking lot.

Recommended:

```text

LARGE BUDDA’S WORDMARK

             ARC FORM 02

small utility
```

One strong mark.

One gesture.

No full menu.

No slogan stack.

No dense botanical field.

## 22.7 Meal Containers

Food integrity outranks permanent print.

Recommended architecture:

- operationally correct neutral / White / Cream container
- Dark Teal or Base Teal label / sleeve

- modifier / order information
- small brand mark

A standardized container plus a strong label system may be more scalable than custom printing every SKU container.

---

## 22.8 Bakery + Single-Roll Packaging

The single Roll is a treat.

It does not need a miniature luxury gift box.

Prefer the minimum package required to protect the product:

- bakery sleeve
- paper pouch
- glassine-style bag
- small clamshell only if product physics require it

Potential Roll sleeve:

- Cream
- Base Teal Script B
- Dark Teal flavor name
- one tiny Arc segment

**DON’T OVERPACKAGE GENEROSITY.**

## 22.9 Cups + Sleeves

## Cups

Keep simple.

Light:

- Cream / White
- Base Teal wordmark

Dark:

- Dark Teal
- Cream wordmark or Script B

Choose one brand mark per face.

## Sleeves

May carry slightly more character:

- Dark Teal + Cream Script B

- small Arc or controlled pattern crop

Still one main idea.

## 22.10 Napkins + Tray Liners

Minimal.

- natural / Cream

- Script B or small wordmark

## Tray Liners

Excellent atmosphere surface.

Potential rotations:

- Island Botanical

- signature discovery
- specific community story

Do not turn the tray liner into a coupon newspaper.

## 22.11 Catering Packaging

Catering has a different emotional job:

**MAKE ME CONFIDENT THIS IS HANDLED.**

Packaging should feel:

- organized
- labeled
- complete
- stackable
- easy to identify
- easy to set up

The Roll Box remains visually privileged.

Recommended hierarchy:

```

One clear insert may solve:

- what is included
- serving guidance
- setup order

- utensils
- support contact
- Roll flavor map if necessary

The organizer should not have to guess.

## 22.12 Delivery + Pickup Packaging

When the employee is absent:

The package must communicate:

- intact
- clean
- complete
- identifiable
- protected

Do not create a seal that technically works while making the package frustrating to open.

## 22.13 Operational Labels

Operational hierarchy:

1. product
2. modifier
3. customer / order
4. date / time where needed
5. safety / handling

No Island Botanical behind tiny operational copy.

- warm
- substantial
- clean
- tactile
- food-safe
- durable enough for the job
- right-sized

- high-gloss luxury finish
- rigid gift-box construction for everyday food
- metallic laminate
- overbuilt layers
- faux-rustic kraft simply to signal “artisan”

Budda’s is:

**MODERN BAKERY + QSR**

not farmhouse craft theater.

```

## 22.16 Sustainability

Do not use unsupported environmental language.

Avoid:

- eco-friendly
- green
- sustainable

unless procurement, materials, and local disposal realities substantiate the claim.

Better design priorities:

- right-size
- reduce layers
- select suitable materials
- simplify mixed-material constructions where practical
- make disposal clear where useful

**SUSTAINABILITY SHOULD BE DESIGNED, NOT PERFORMED.**

- folding
- food fit
- grease
- condensation
- heat
- stackability
- grip
- delivery
- assembly time
- substrate color
- opening experience

## 22.18 Production QA

Every production package should check:

- dieline version
- bleed
- folds
- glue zones
- copy
- QR / barcode quiet zones
- food-safe inks / coatings where applicable
- variable label areas
- image resolution
- color target
- substrate
- finish
- legal marks
- storage / assembly behavior

Packaging errors scale expensively.

---

## 22.19 Physical Brand Tests

## Across-the-Table Test

At ten feet, with the text unreadable, does the Roll Box still feel like Budda’s?

## Logo-Off Test

Cover the wordmark.

Does form + color + Arc + food + interior behavior still create recognition?

## Pattern-Off Test

Remove Island Botanical.

The package must still work.

## Premium Test

Does it feel better because it is controlled—or merely because more finishing effects were added?

## Waste Test

Would the customer value this extra layer?

If not:

remove it.

# 25. Accessibility + Inclusive Experience

## 25.1 Digital Baseline

**WCAG 2.2 AA**

is the minimum digital target.

Accessibility is not an appendix to Budda’s hospitality.

It is hospitality without friction applied to a wider range of guests.

---

## 25.2 Text Contrast

Digital minimums used by this system:

- normal text: 4.5:1
- qualifying large text: 3:1

Use the locked color contrast matrix rather than assumptions.

The official wordmark is legally treated differently from normal text under WCAG contrast criteria, but Budda’s should still choose the clearest approved treatment in each context.

---

## 25.3 Non-Text Contrast

Meaningful controls, focus indicators, and interface graphics must maintain sufficient visual contrast against adjacent colors.

Do not use:

- subtle tonal Teal-on-Teal for critical controls
- Gold on Cream for required wayfinding
- low-opacity borders as the only input boundary when the control becomes difficult to identify

---

## 25.4 Target Size

WCAG 2.2 AA includes a 24×24 CSS px minimum target-size criterion with defined exceptions / spacing provisions.

Budda’s preferred design standard should exceed that where practical:

```text
Primary button height      48–56px
Standard input height      48–52px
Icon control target        44×44px preferred
Navigation item height     ~44px+ effective
```

The visual icon may remain 16–24px inside the larger control.

---

## 25.5 Focus

Use `:focus-visible`.

Required:

- visible focus indicator
- enough contrast
- not clipped
- not entirely obscured by sticky / fixed content
- predictable focus return after modal / drawer close

Recommended baseline:

- 2–3px ring
- 2px offset where useful

Never globally remove outlines without a fully equivalent replacement.

---

## 25.6 Keyboard

All standard digital actions should remain keyboard operable.

Required behaviors:

- logical tab sequence
- Enter / Space according to native semantics
- Escape closes dismissible modal / drawer where safe
- no keyboard traps
- menus and disclosures follow conventional patterns

Use native HTML controls before custom ARIA-heavy implementations.

---

## 25.7 Forms

Every form control needs:

- visible label
- programmatic label
- clear instructions
- clear required state
- error associated to field
- non-color-only error cue
- preserved user input after validation errors

Placeholder text is supplemental.

It is not a label.

---

## 25.8 Semantic Structure

Use the correct element for the job:

- link = navigation
- button = action
- list = list
- table = tabular relationships
- headings = page hierarchy
- `nav`, `main`, `footer` = landmarks
- form elements = native controls

Do not create clickable `<div>` systems because they are easier to style.

---

## 25.9 Headings

Each page should have one clear primary heading context.

Do not choose heading tags based on visual size.

Visual style and semantic rank are separate concerns.

---

## 25.10 Image Alternatives

### Decorative

Ignore semantically / use empty alt.

### Product

Describe the food and the meaningful feature.

### Story / Documentary

Describe the relevant action, person, or setting.

### Diagram / Data

Provide equivalent information in text.

### Text in Image

Avoid when actual HTML can provide the content.

---

## 25.11 Motion Accessibility

`prefers-reduced-motion` is a first-class state.

Reduced mode removes:

- Arc sweeps
- parallax
- large translations
- decorative looping motion
- scale-based section choreography

It preserves:

- state clarity
- confirmation
- navigation
- content hierarchy

---

## 25.12 Zoom + Reflow

At high text zoom and narrow reflow:

- no clipped copy
- no overlapping controls
- no fixed-height content traps
- no hidden actions
- no horizontal scrolling for normal reading content

Use flexible block sizing.

---

## 25.13 Language

Keep utility language plain.

Do not replace universal words like:

- Pickup
- Restrooms
- Order
- Exit

with branded metaphors that increase cognitive load.

Warmth comes through voice and behavior, not semantic obscurity.

---

## 25.14 Physical Accessibility Boundary

This document sets brand and experience intent.

It does **not** replace local architectural, building-code, fire, health, accessibility, or life-safety review.

Every restaurant, remodel, kiosk, sign system, counter, restroom, path, and seating plan must be reviewed against the requirements applicable to that location by qualified professionals.

Brand expression may never be used to justify reduced accessibility.

---

## 25.15 Physical Inclusive Principles

Regardless of exact jurisdictional dimensions:

- order paths should be obvious
- accessible service points should feel integrated, not secondary
- signs should have strong contrast and be placed at useful decision points
- seating should support a range of guests
- circulation should not depend on navigating decorative obstacles
- digital kiosks should follow the same accessible UI rules as the web
- audio cannot be the only way an order status is communicated
- visual cannot be the only way when an accessible alternative is required

**INCLUSION SHOULD FEEL LIKE THE NORMAL EXPERIENCE.**

# 26. Performance + Technical Experience

## 26.1 Performance North Star

**A PREMIUM EXPERIENCE SHOULD FEEL IMMEDIATE.**

Food photography and motion are valuable.

They do not have unlimited performance budgets.

---

## 26.2 Core Web Vitals Production Targets

Current “good” thresholds used by this system:

```text
LCP ≤ 2.5 seconds
INP ≤ 200 milliseconds
CLS ≤ 0.1
```

Evaluate real-user performance at the 75th percentile separately where appropriate across mobile and desktop.

---

## 26.3 Hero Media

Do not:

- send 4K stills to narrow mobile displays
- autoplay a multi-megabyte cinematic video simply because a hero exists
- lazy-load the likely LCP image
- hide the H1 / CTA until a decorative animation ends
- load separate desktop and mobile media simultaneously when responsive markup can select one

---

## 26.4 Responsive Images

Use:

- `srcset`
- `sizes`
- `<picture>` for genuine art direction
- explicit width / height or `aspect-ratio`
- efficient modern formats where appropriate
- source sizes close to actual rendered dimensions

For Tier A hero production, plan sources / crops for:

- 21:9 safe
- 16:9
- 4:5
- 9:16

---

## 26.5 Image Priority

### High Priority

- initial visible product hero
- critical logo SVG
- immediate navigation graphics

### Lazy

- below-fold galleries
- story photography
- secondary product media
- offscreen carousels

---

## 26.6 CLS

Reserve media geometry before it loads.

Avoid:

- images without dimensions
- late-loading banners that push the page
- font swaps with large layout shift where avoidable
- sticky elements that suddenly change page geometry

---

## 26.7 Font Delivery

Load only the locked families / needed weights.

Recommended:

- optimized self-hosting or reliable performance-controlled delivery
- critical weights only
- reasonable fallback metrics
- official script as vector artwork, not a webfont imitation

Do not download every Poppins / DM Sans weight because the files exist.

---

## 26.8 JavaScript Discipline

A visually branded component does not automatically need a JavaScript animation library.

Prefer:

1. native HTML
2. CSS
3. platform APIs
4. lightweight component logic
5. heavier animation / interaction libraries only when value justifies cost

---

## 26.9 Animation Performance

Prefer:

- transform
- opacity
- efficient SVG animation for Arc / Table / mark

Avoid continuous animation of:

- layout dimensions
- expensive filters
- large blur values
- JavaScript-driven position loops

---

## 26.10 Video

Autoplay food loops, when justified:

- muted
- short
- clean loop
- useful poster image
- reduced-motion fallback
- not required for comprehension
- loaded only where the communication value justifies the bandwidth

---

## 26.11 Performance Review

A component review should ask:

> What customer value does this media / motion produce per byte and per millisecond?

If the answer is mostly:

> it looks cool,

simplify it.

# 27. Component Foundations

Components are not miniature brand posters.

A component must first perform its job.

The identity creates consistency around that job.

---

## 27.1 Universal Component Requirements

Every interactive component must define:

- default
- hover where relevant
- pressed
- focus-visible
- selected where relevant
- disabled where relevant
- loading where relevant
- error / success where relevant
- responsive behavior
- reduced-motion behavior when motion exists
- semantic HTML / ARIA contract

---

## 27.2 Buttons

## Primary

Use for the most important action in a decision region.

Recommended:

- Dark Teal fill
- Cream text
- Poppins SemiBold
- 48–56px height
- 20–28px horizontal padding
- 12–16px radius
- no pill by default

Motion:

- hover 140–180ms
- press 80–120ms
- optional `translateY(-1px)` hover
- optional `translateY(1px)` or scale `.985` press

---

## Secondary

- Cream / transparent field
- Dark Teal text
- Dark Teal border
- same visual height as paired Primary

---

## Tertiary

Text / directional action.

Use when the action is clearly lower priority.

---

## Promotional

Orange only when the action is genuinely tied to a temporary promotion, opening, or LTO.

---

## Button Copy

Prefer specific verbs:

- Order Online
- View Menu
- Find a Location
- Start Catering Order
- Request Information

Avoid:

- Click Here
- Submit
- Learn More where a more specific verb is available

---

## 27.3 Links

Links should look like navigation, not normal body copy.

In paragraph contexts, provide persistent or reliably visible text-decoration / affordance beyond color alone.

External-link icons are useful only when leaving the current experience is materially relevant.

---

## 27.4 Inputs

Baseline:

- 48–52px height
- 16px text minimum
- 12–16px radius
- visible label
- Dark Teal / Cocoa content
- strong focus state

Use White when dense utility benefits from it.

Use Cream where emotional brand context remains valuable.

---

## 27.5 Select / Combobox

Use native select where it satisfies the experience.

If a custom combobox is needed:

- follow conventional keyboard behavior
- maintain clear expanded state
- active option visible
- selection persistent
- no decorative motion that slows scanning

---

## 27.6 Checkbox + Radio

Controls should be clearly visible at rest.

Selected state requires:

- shape / check / dot
- not color alone
- adequate control / label target

Do not redraw them into food-themed icons.

---

## 27.7 Cards

Do not force every job into one generic Card.

Core families:

### Product Card

Food → name → description → price / action.

### Story Card

Image / metadata → title → story cue.

### Utility Card

Information first.

### Evidence Card

Claim / number → context → evidence.

### Location Card

Place → status → hours / address → actions.

---

## 27.8 Product Cards

Rules:

- food is visually dominant
- Dark Teal product name
- Cocoa description
- price clear but not louder than product by default
- minimal decorative geometry
- unavailable state persistent
- dietary / option markers use Utility Line conventions

Do not place Arc Form 02 on every card.

---

## 27.9 Accordions

Use for:

- FAQ
- supporting process detail
- catering information
- franchise disclosures

Requirements:

- whole header is a real button
- `aria-expanded`
- persistent open / closed cue
- 220–280ms standard motion
- core conversion information should not be hidden just to make the page shorter

---

## 27.10 Tabs

Use only for peer groups that benefit from immediate switching.

Do not use tabs to disguise a long story sequence.

Selected state should remain clear beyond color alone.

---

## 27.11 Carousels

Use when horizontal browsing is genuinely helpful.

Requirements:

- accessible previous / next controls
- current position / selected item visible where relevant
- no auto-advance for essential information
- auto motion, when used, must be pausable and nonessential
- swipe is supplemental, not the only input

---

## 27.12 Dialogs

Use for focused interruption.

Requirements:

- initial focus placed intentionally
- focus contained / background inert where appropriate
- Escape closes where safe
- visible close control
- focus returns logically
- 220–300ms motion

---

## 27.13 Drawers

Use for:

- cart
- mobile navigation
- contextual filters

Open:

260–340ms.

Close:

slightly faster.

Utility first.

---

## 27.14 Tooltips

Only use for secondary clarification.

Do not hide required labels in tooltips.

Timing:

160–220ms.

Must be available through keyboard focus as well as pointer hover.

---

## 27.15 Toasts

Use for transient confirmation.

Do not put critical, unrecoverable errors only in a toast that disappears.

Keep language concise and actionable.

---

## 27.16 Badges + Chips

Use for real metadata:

- NEW
- LIMITED
- category
- selected filter
- availability

Do not use seven badges as decoration.

Orange belongs only to genuine temporary emphasis.

---

## 27.17 Tables

Use tables for actual tabular relationships.

Requirements:

- headers
- caption / context where helpful
- readable type
- responsive strategy
- horizontal scrolling only when table structure truly matters

Do not fragment financial or comparison data into dozens of cards if the table relationship is easier to understand.

---

## 27.18 Data Visualization

Budda’s is not a data-dashboard brand by default.

When charts are needed in franchise / reporting contexts:

- Cream / White surface
- Dark Teal primary structure
- Base Teal secondary series
- Gold sparingly
- Orange only for relevant alert / temporary emphasis
- label directly where possible
- do not use decorative 3D charts
- include source / period context

Evidence outranks drama.

Prompt description is secondary to actual product evidence.

Actual photographed product / place / person.

### AI-ASSISTED

Reference-faithful generated / composited asset approved for specified use.

### CONCEPT ONLY

Not approved as representation of currently available product, packaging, or environment.

### APPROVED FUTURE

Production-authorized future asset that may be used only according to launch timing / disclosure.

AI-generated cultural storytelling requires the same review standard as human-created cultural storytelling.

# 38. Governance + Change Control

## 38.1 Purpose

A scalable brand needs controlled evolution.

The goal is:

**CONTROL WITHOUT SUFFOCATION.**

---

## 38.2 Change Classes

### Patch

Bug fix / implementation correction.

Examples:

- contrast correction
- broken state
- spacing bug

### Minor

New reusable component / semantic token / approved application.

Examples:

- new date-picker pattern
- new verified Product Marker

### Major

Change to:

- official logo
- descriptor
- master palette
- type family
- Arc geometry
- Table Line definition
- master pattern
- Product Truth standard
- core identity mode
- packaging / environment North Star

Requires upstream brand review and version update.

---

## 38.3 Review Owners

Major work should have appropriate participation from:

- Brand / Creative
- Digital / Product Design
- Engineering
- Operations
- Food / Product Truth
- Accessibility / QA
- Packaging / production where physical
- Architecture / environment where physical
- qualified cultural reviewer where culturally meaningful content is introduced

Not every minor UI adjustment needs every reviewer.

Governance should match risk.

---

## 38.4 Decision Rule

When two good-looking choices conflict, ask in order:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it Budda’s?
3. Does the food remain desirable?
4. Does it make the guest’s job easier?
5. Does it show care?
6. Can it be reproduced at scale?

---

## 38.5 Brand Source of Truth

The operating brand library should eventually govern:

- official name
- descriptor
- locations
- hours
- product names
- menu descriptions
- photography
- claims
- founder / origin story
- packaging
- environment
- social identity
- franchise language

A beautiful system fails if public truth remains fragmented.

System for information.

Structure, not decoration.

Product Truth before style.

Utility Line remains conventional enough to understand and distinctive enough to belong.

## 11 — ILLUSTRATION CREATES UNDERSTANDING.

Real story. Flat geometry. No cultural shortcuts.

Warm motion.

Controlled landing.

Function moves faster than branding.

## 13 — PACKAGING IS GENEROUSLY REVEALED.

Controlled outside.

Expressive inside.

The package serves the food.

## 14 — THE BAKERY OPENS INTO THE TABLE.

The restaurant begins with craving and ends in belonging.

Specificity before cliché.

Better photography.

More locations, pages, vendors, and operators should create more Budda’s—not more versions of Budda’s.
